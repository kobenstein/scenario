# 01 · Superintelligence Is Rising. Here's How We Get It Right.

Flagship episode. Replaces `episodes/01-intelligence-explosion`. Research: `/mnt/project-files/scenario/folge-01-flagship/` (`takeoff-now.md`, `structure.md`, `sources.md`).
Jakob's outline (2026-10-17 22:01): the takeoff has started → what that means → why steering now matters → how we steer (longest) → where it leads and your part. The ending reads like a manifesto.

## Foundation

1. **Focus sentence:** Humanity is building a technology that has started to help build itself, because the gains are enormous, *but* our ability to see inside and check it is growing more slowly than what it can do. *Therefore* the next few years decide whether we keep the steering.
   Blumberg test: this is a story about the AI takeoff. What's interesting is that the people closest to it are not asking *whether* we can steer, but *how*, and they largely agree on the answer.
2. **The question behind the question:** not "When will superintelligence arrive?" but "Will we still be able to see where we're going when it does?"
3. **The one insight:** The takeoff isn't humans against machines. It's two curves, what AI can do and what we can see and check, and getting it right means keeping the second close to the first. *Next headline:* every "lab X released model Y" story makes the viewer ask "and who checked it?"
4. **The misconception:** "Superintelligence is a moment, a product launch, a switch." Broken by evidence that it's a runway, a loop that is already turning (S1, S2, S6).
5. **Leitmotif:** *Headlights in fog.* "Never drive faster than you can see." It explains the mechanism (the gap between the two curves) and points to the way out: you don't stop the car, you bring the headlights ahead of the speed. The four solutions are the four ways to do that. Supporting image: the runway (the takeoff is still on the ground).
6. **People:** the figure ("you", small, amber) on the San Francisco street from the motion test, at night in the hook and at dawn in 2035. Real voices: Jared Kaplan, Neel Nanda, Dario Amodei, Yann LeCun, Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor. The New Delhi signatories stand in for the Global South (S85); this is still thin.
7. **Value:** stewardship. Agency over our shared future ("we still have the wheel").

## The feeling Jakob asked for: a turning point in human history
- Scale shift in part 3: 300,000 years of Homo sapiens → 5,000 years of writing → *now* the first tool that helps design its own successor.
- "Not because superintelligence is here. Because the decisions about it are."
- Kaplan's "perhaps the biggest decision" (S15) and the Collingridge dilemma (T4): we are in the narrow stretch where the technology is still young enough to shape.
- Finale: "We are living through a turning point that most generations never get to see. We are not passengers."
- No fear: the threat is always the *gap*, never the machine, and it comes with a working answer in the same act.
