## 6 · How we get there (7:10–10:15)

> First: treat near misses like aviation does.
↳ The workbench becomes an old paper airport at dawn. Chapter word: REPORT.

> In nineteen seventy-four, a TWA jet flew into a mountain near Washington. Six weeks earlier, a United crew had made the same mistake, and pulled up just in time. [S14]
↳ Two paper planes on the same path toward a dark ridge; the first lifts at the last second, the second doesn't. No crash shown, the second plane simply fades into the ridge's shadow.

> Nobody else ever heard about it.
↳ The United plane's tiny pilot holds a paper note that never leaves the cockpit.

> So in nineteen seventy-six, America built a system where pilots report their own mistakes, confidentially and without punishment. [S14]
↳ A paper mailbox at the airport; notes fly into it from many tiny cockpits.

> More than two million reports have come in since. Reporting is one reason flying became so safe, alongside better technology and training. [S14]
↳ The mailbox grows into a paper archive; planes overhead fly in calm, gentle arcs.

> AI has taken a first step. After Hugging Face disclosed the July breach, OpenAI came forward to say its own models did it. [S2]
↳ The archive cross-dissolves via its drawers into the Hugging Face tower, drawers now being catalogued by amber figures.

> California now requires frontier AI developers to report critical safety incidents within fifteen days. [S15]
↳ A paper calendar tears off fifteen pages in a quick flutter.

> The limits: the law's definition is narrow, many near misses fall outside it, and outsiders still haven't seen the full logs of July. [S15] [S16]
↳ A sieve of paper; most small notes fall through; one big note is caught.

> Second: let someone else grade the test.
↳ The exam booklet from the hook returns; a different hand, amber, picks up the red pencil. Chapter word: TEST.

> The July agents tried to cheat on an exam. And that "research intern" milestone? OpenAI graded it itself. [S7]

> Government testers in Britain and the US now get early access to many frontier models before release. [S17]
↳ Amber inspectors at a paper gate between the lab and the city; models pass through one at a time.

↳ A long straight paper road at sunrise; workers assemble the brake lever from earlier. Chapter word: BRAKE.

> We've done this before. In nineteen seventy-four, biologists who had just learned to splice DNA asked their own field to pause certain experiments. And the pause held. [S19]
↳ Paper lab benches; tiny scientists set their pipettes down at once.

> At Asilomar, in California, about a hundred and fifty people, mostly scientists, agreed on rules matching each kind of experiment to a level of containment. [S19]

> Research went on, and grew into biotech.
↳ The boxes open into a garden of paper plants and medicine bottles in morning gold.

↳ The road splits into several roads, each with its own tower; hands from every tower reach toward one shared lever.

↳ A single paper chip, glowing, sends a thin ping out to beacons around a paper globe.

↳ Paper bills stacked on a desk under a dome; a stamp hovers, not yet pressed.
