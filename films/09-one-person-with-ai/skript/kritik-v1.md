# One-Person Film · Retention critique of Script v1

Critic's pass, 2026-10-22. I read it as a viewer on a phone, half distracted, who clicked a thumbnail with a kitchen table and a glass tower. Timings use the script's own method (words ÷ 145 + clip + pauses). Checked against hook-bauplan.md, vibe-brief.md, feedback-log.md, geruest-v3.md and faktenzettel.md.

## Verdict

1. The core idea is strong and new: "Artificial intelligence isn't an equalizer. It's an amplifier." The guide format, the kitchen table, "so can everyone else" and World B are vision, not report. The ending has the right shape, with no subscribe plea and a real comment question.
2. It leaks in three places. **(a)** The hook passes the Gegentest only half-way: the clip comes without its question, a 21-word report sentence explains it afterwards, and the roadmap promises only the three steps, not the fork and solutions that make up 60 % of the film. **(b)** 1:48–3:22 slips into report tone, with nine numbers in 95 seconds, and that is why the big question arrives at 3:22. **(c)** The solutions (5:02–8:35) read like a policy brief. The same template runs four times ("X offers… The World Bank argues… What travels… The limit…"), "you" disappears for three and a half minutes, and every block ends on its limit.
3. No full rewrite is needed. Fixes 1–3 below bring the fork to about **2:44** and make the first minute pass all four Gegentest checks.

### Gegentest (first 60 s, as written)

| Check | Pass? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (a) What is it about? | Mostly | "Let's build a billion-dollar company" is clear. But the title says *beat* a billion-dollar company and the hook says *build* one. These are close, but not the same promise (see fix 2). |
| (b) Why is it real? | Half | The clip plays as a bare "2026" with no question, so the ear doesn't know what the number answers. The context comes afterwards as a 21-word report sentence, and then "To be fair, it hasn't happened yet" lets the air out right after the proof. |
| (c) Which question is open? | Yes, late | "If it works, who wins?" arrives at ~0:37 (the target is 20–35 s), behind a stock warm-up ("here's the question almost nobody asks"). |
| (d) What do I get by the end? | No | "Three steps. Team, product, growth." That is the contract. The fork and the four answers (~60 % of the runtime) were never promised, so the viewer who came for the guide feels the film change topic at 3:22. |

## Top 12 fixes, ranked by retention impact

### 1 · Hook: set up the clip, turn the objection into tension, plant "millions" (0:00–0:37)
**Quoted:**
> Not to the people building this technology.
> [CLIP: Amodei, "2026"]
> That's the head of one of the leading artificial intelligence labs, asked when one person alone will build a billion-dollar company.
> To be fair, it hasn't happened yet.
> But here's the question almost nobody asks.
> If it works, who wins?

**Problem:** The proof is the strongest moment in the first minute, but it fires blind. A bare "2026" means nothing until the 21-word sentence afterwards (the longest line in the hook, and pure source-says-X). Then "To be fair, it hasn't happened yet" sounds like the narrator taking the proof back. In fact, the fair objection hides the best tension in the film: he said it *last year* about *this year*, and the year is almost over. "Here's the question almost nobody asks" is a 3-second announcement. "Who wins?" is abstract. "If one person can, millions can" is the film's real twist, and planting it here makes the fork feel like a payoff.

**Rewrite (73 words, open question at ~0:33):**
> Let's build a billion-dollar company.
> Just you, a kitchen table, and artificial intelligence.
> No office. No staff. No investors.
> Sounds like a fantasy?
> Not to the people building this technology.
> [CLIP: Amodei, ORIGINAL FOOTAGE. The interviewer's question as an on-screen text card ("When will the first one-person billion-dollar company appear?"), then his answer: "2026"]
> That's the head of one of the biggest artificial intelligence labs.
> He said it last year. He meant this one.
> Nobody has done it yet.
> But if one person can, so can millions.
> And then, who wins?
> Just you?
> Or all of us?

The fact is still honest (said 22.05.2025, answer "2026", no such company exists). The narrator never repeats "2026". "He meant this one" only works if the film goes out in 2026. If it slips into 2027, use "He said it in twenty twenty-five. Nobody has done it yet."

### 2 · Roadmap promises only half the film; the first win is the weaker fact (0:37–1:00)
**Quoted:**
> At each step, you'll see why this is suddenly possible, and where it breaks.
> Step one has already happened to someone.
> A founder in Austin got laid off. [S7]
> His new company, it says, runs on three people and twelve artificial intelligence agents. [S7]

**Problem:** (1) Gegentest (d) fails, as shown above. Add one line that promises the second half. (2) The hook plan wants a *surprising* concrete fact at 0:50. "Three people and twelve agents" is a mild one. The hedge "it says" sounds like a reporter, and "got laid off" is biography. The film owns a much stronger first win: Base44, which is literally the title's promise in miniature (one person, a side project, six months, a buyer). Its honest tail ("by then, a small team") also stays fair. (3) The title says *beat*, the hook says *build*. Either align the title with the hook (e.g. "One Person, One Billion-Dollar Company?") or keep the glass tower visibly *opposite* the table in the first frame, so "against the giants" is in the picture even if it isn't in the words.

**Rewrite (65 words, ends ~1:00):**
> So let's build it, in three steps.
> Your team. Your product. Your growth.
> At each step: why it's suddenly possible, and where it breaks.
> And then, what happens when millions follow the same guide.
> One developer started a tool called Base44, alone, as a side project.
> About six months later, Wix bought it for around eighty million dollars.
> By then, he had a small team.

That is the one spoken number in the minute, after 0:50, which is allowed. Fathom is dropped, or kept as a 2-line proof in step 1 if there's time (see fix 3).

### 4 · Step one: a 1937 economist in the middle of your kitchen scene (1:15–1:30)
**Quoted:**
> We used to think building something real takes money and a lot of people.
> The economist Ronald Coase explained why: coordinating people, task by task, is expensive. [S3]
> Make working together cheap, and you need less company. [S3]
> That gives you closeness.

**Problem:** The scene ("One answers your customers at three in the morning") is the best vision in the first two minutes, and then it hands the mic to a dead economist. That is source-says-X and past-looking, both on Jakob's no-list. "You need less company" trips the ear (less *of a* company? fewer companions?). "That gives you closeness" is a literal translation of "Nähe" and means nothing in English.

**Rewrite:**
> Companies got big because working together was expensive.
> Now it's getting cheap.
> And that means you can go small.
> You can build for a problem only forty people have.

Coase can appear as a name card in the picture (glass tower dissolving into threads) if Jakob wants the credit.

### 5 · Step two: five numbers in 33 seconds, no "you" (1:48–2:21)
**Quoted:**
> More than one in three new startups now has a single founder. [S4]
> One developer started a tool called Base44 alone, as a side project. [S6]
> About half a year later, Wix bought it for around eighty million dollars, by then with a small team. [S6]
> In the first half of this year, the App Store got almost twice as many new apps as a year earlier. [S8]
> Downloads grew by just two percent. [S8]

**Problem:** This is the report-tone peak before the fork: one in three, half a year, eighty million, twice, two percent. "You" vanishes from your own step. The Carta line also overreaches in context: it's Carta companies only, and the trend began before ChatGPT, but placed under "build it with AI" it implies cause. The App Store sentence is 21 words with the punchline buried at the end.

**Rewrite (48 words):**
> Step two. Build it, in weeks.
> Your edge is speed.
> What a company plans for a quarter, you can ship by Friday.
> But building got cheap.
> Attention didn't.
> In the first half of this year, the App Store got almost twice as many new apps.
> Downloads barely moved.

The "ship by Friday" line is part of the marked thought experiment, not a fact claim. The "+2 %" goes in the picture (the waterfall of icons).

### 6 · Step three: the best fact in the film, told like a study abstract (2:21–2:55)
**Quoted:**
> In Kenya, researchers gave hundreds of small business owners an artificial intelligence adviser on WhatsApp. [S11]
> On average, nothing changed. [S11]
> But those already doing well earned over fifteen percent more. [S11]
> Those who were struggling earned about ten percent less. [S11]
> Similar advice. Different choices about what to use. [S11]

**Problem:** "Does that make you better?" is a great setup, because the viewer makes a guess. Then the answer comes as three results with two percentages, which sounds like a paper's abstract. "On average, nothing changed" is a third result the ear has to hold. "Different choices about what to use" is abstract, while the fact sheet has the concrete detail (the struggling picked generic tips like cutting prices).

**Rewrite:**
> In Kenya, hundreds of small business owners got exactly that: an artificial intelligence adviser on WhatsApp.
> Food stalls. Poultry farms. Internet cafés.
> The ones already doing well earned more.
> The ones who were struggling earned less.
> The advice was similar.
> But the struggling ones picked the easy tips, like cutting prices.
> Artificial intelligence isn't an equalizer.
> It's an amplifier.
> And your team is rented.
> So yes, you have a real chance against a corporation.
> Just not an equal one.

Put "+15 % / −10 %" on the split screen (poultry farm brightens, café dims). Don't speak them.

### 7 · The fork: three statistics right after the big question (3:30–3:50), and a flat proof that ends World B
**Quoted:**
> We're closer to this world than it sounds.
> In America, the smallest businesses already use artificial intelligence less than big ones, and that gap is widening. [S12]
> Worldwide, about one working person in four uses it in the richer North. [S13]
> In the South, it's one in seven, and that gap is growing too. [S13]
> …
> The seeds are already there.
> One service for new founders signs up companies from one hundred and sixty-nine countries. [S5]

**Problem:** The viewer has just been handed the film's biggest question, and the narrator answers with a census table. "Closer than it sounds" is the only line in the film that leans toward alarm. "One service for new founders signs up companies from…" is the weakest sentence in the film: a vague subject, a report verb and a number, placed right after the most sensory passage. It deflates World B instead of crowning it.

**Rewrite (World A, no numbers, with the ratios on screen):**
> And we're already drifting this way.
> The smallest businesses use these tools least.
> So do the poorer half of the world's workers.
> Both gaps are growing.

**World B:** end on "They become the roads everyone drives on." Cut "The seeds are already there" and the 169 countries. Also "her slowest student" → "the student who keeps falling behind" (kinder, and easier to picture).

### 8 · The four answers sound like a policy brief. Make them the road to World B (5:02–8:35)
**Quoted (pattern, ×4):**
> India offers public computing power… OpenAI gave people in India… Britain offers free artificial intelligence courses…
> What travels is the idea: treat computing power like a road, something the public builds so everyone can use it.
> The limit: nobody has shown yet that these programmes work.
> …The limit: it's one country, one policy, and it costs public money.

**Problem:** This is the longest part (3:33) and the one Jakob will judge hardest, because it's the "how", and right now it is a list. Four identical blocks (label → country facts → "What travels" → "The limit") tell the ear exactly when each block ends, which invites the thumb. "What travels" is internal jargon, not spoken English. "You" disappears from 5:10 to 8:25. Worse, every block *ends on its limit*, which gives four small deflations in a row. The vision is already in the film: the nurse, the farmer and the teacher from World B. Use them.

**Fix:** Each answer opens on one World B person ("How does the farmer get there?"), gives one proof line, puts the limit *in the middle* in one clause, and ends on the person again. Drop "What travels" everywhere. Drop the OpenAI free year (a third programme, it sounds like a promo, and it's the third source-says line in a row).

**Rewrite example, answer two (~65 words, was ~75):**
> Two. Access for everyone.
> The teacher in India, building that exercise?
> She needs computing power she can afford.
> India now offers it to startups and small firms for less than a dollar an hour.
> Britain offers every adult a free course, aiming for ten million workers by twenty thirty.
> Does it work? Nobody knows yet. It's a bet.
> But it's the right kind of bet.
> Treat computing power like a road.
> The public builds it. Everyone drives on it.

This harvests World B's "roads everyone drives on". Answer 1 opens on the farmer (Kenya mentoring + the struggling owners) and answer 3 on the nurse (her rota tool can't be switched off, fix 9). Answer 4 turns the camera to you: "And you? What would it take for *you* to jump?"

### 9 · Answer three: 40 seconds without a story, a 20-word principle list (6:50–7:30)
**Quoted:**
> So the many need open models, the freedom to switch providers, and fair rules for the platforms everyone depends on.
> The World Bank argues for small, cheap tools that work by text message, even on a basic phone. [S18]
> And it warns against depending on a single provider. [S18]

**Problem:** This is the one stretch of over 40 s where nothing new happens to a person: a manifesto line, two "the World Bank says" lines, then a limit. "Five days' notice" lands here as a callback to a story told four minutes earlier. Move the Windsurf story here and it becomes the scene this answer is missing. Step 3 keeps only the plant "Your team is rented."

**Rewrite:**
> Three. Tools nobody can switch off.
> Remember: your team is rented.
> One startup, Windsurf, lost direct access to the models it depended on.
> With less than five days' notice.
> The landlord was the company from that first clip.
> Five days is fine for a hobby. Not for a livelihood.
> So the nurse's rota tool should run on a model nobody can take away.
> And on any phone. Even the cheapest one.
> Open tools still cost money to run, and fair rules take years to write.
> But a tool that works on the cheapest phone works for almost everyone.

The World Bank can be a quote card in the picture ("small AI"). The "remember" tic: the script already has two ("Remember: everyone can hire…", "Remember the owners in Kenya…"), so keep at most two in the film.

### 10 · The ending lands twice, so it lands softer (8:25–9:20)
**Quoted:**
> All four answers share one idea.
> The guide said: you, alone.
> The many win when nobody has to do it alone.
> Which brings us back to the kitchen table.
> The guide had a missing step.
> …The missing step decides who else gets to follow the guide.

**Problem:** "The many win when nobody has to do it alone" is the real payoff of the film, and it fires at 8:33, inside the solutions. Ten seconds later the ending reveals "the missing step", which is the same idea again but more abstract ("decides who else gets to follow the guide" makes the ear do logic). "That was never the interesting question" tells the viewer who clicked for the title that their question was boring. "So, one last question, just for you." is a warm-up.

**Rewrite (§5 ends on "And you decide too: who you teach, who you hire, whose tool you try."):**
> Which brings us back to the kitchen table.
> The guide had three steps.
> Team. Build. Grow.
> It was missing one.
> The guide said: you, alone.
> But the many win when nobody has to do it alone.
> That step isn't on your kitchen table.
> It's on ours.
> [pause]
> Maybe one person really will build a billion-dollar company soon.
> But the bigger question is how many people get to try.
> Not one light against a tower.
> Millions of them.
> [pause]
> So, one last question.

### 11 · Answer four: "a basic income in miniature" is a claim the facts don't cover (7:40–8:05)
**Quoted:**
> When France let unemployed people keep their benefits while starting a company, many more people started one. [S19]
> It's a basic income in miniature.

**Problem:** [S19] is about unemployed people keeping their unemployment benefits while they found a company. That is a temporary, conditional safety net, not a basic income. A Film 4/5 viewer will notice, and so will a commenter. It also reaches for the channel's other films instead of staying in this one. The rest of answer 4 is the most human block in the solutions ("Not because they're timid. Because they have rent to pay."), so keep it.

**Rewrite:**
> It's a safety net you keep while you try.

Then cut the separate "What travels: let people keep their safety net while they try." line, which now repeats it.

### 12 · Ear and tic sweep (whole script)
- **Sentences over 18 words.** All of them are fixed above except: "Britain offers free artificial intelligence courses to every adult, aiming to train ten million workers by twenty thirty." (18, fixed in fix 8) and "What travels is the idea: treat computing power like a road, something the public builds so everyone can use it." (20, fixed in fix 8).
- **Report hedges:** "His new company, it says, runs on…" → "by its own count" (if Fathom stays). "Kenya also shows what does help." → "Something did help."
- **Stiff for the ear:** "whom you teach, whom you hire" → "who you teach, who you hire". "less than a dollar an hour" is fine spoken, but the fact sheet says per GPU-hour, so show "per GPU-hour" on screen.
- **Announcements:** "Here's the real answer to the question we started with." → "So here's the answer." "So, one last question, just for you." → "So, one last question."
- **Same image twice:** "Not one tower. Millions of small workshops." (World B) and "Not one light against a tower. Millions of them." (ending). Keep the ending one and change World B to "Millions of small workshops, each run by someone who knows a problem by heart." (drop "Not one tower.").
- **Mild startup-bro register:** "Your edge is speed." is fine once. Don't add "crush", "scale", "hustle" anywhere in revisions.
- **Machine imagery:** "twelve artificial intelligence agents" is the only counted-machines line, and it's gone with the Fathom swap. The picture lines are all human. Good.
- **Fear check:** nothing alarming in the VO after fix 7. In the picture, "the plug is pulled, the table goes dark for a beat" in §3 now moves with Windsurf to answer 3, where the next beat (the cable splitting into many) resolves it. Keep it there.

## Keep: this works

- **The core insight:** "Artificial intelligence isn't an equalizer. It's an amplifier." It's clear in one breath, surprising, and comment-worthy.
- **The guide format and "Let's build a billion-dollar company. Just you, a kitchen table, and artificial intelligence."** The title promise is in the first 5 s and "you" by the second line.
- **"Sounds like a fantasy? Not to the people building this technology."** A clean bridge into the clip.
- **The step-1 scene:** "And around you, quietly, a team appears… One answers your customers at three in the morning." Vision, not report.
- **"A problem only forty people have. For a corporation, that's nothing. For you, it's a start."** It's planted here and harvested in the comment question.
- **"But if you can hire this team in an afternoon, so can everyone else."** The best plant in the script, harvested at the fork.
- **"But building got cheap. Attention didn't."**
- **"Does that make you better?"** It makes the viewer guess before the reveal.
- **"Now imagine you're not the only one following this guide. Millions are."** A great turn.
- **World B:** the nurse's rota tool, the farmer's basic phone, "Millions of small workshops, each run by someone who knows a problem by heart", and "the giants don't disappear. They become the roads everyone drives on." These are people, not robots, and the future, not the past.
- **"So pause here. Which world would you bet on?"** Then: "The technology doesn't decide this. The same tools lead to both worlds. A few choices do." That second reveal is Jakob's thesis in five seconds.
- **"Not because they're timid. Because they have rent to pay."** The warmest line in the solutions.
- **The responsibility list** (labs, governments, schools, investors, you). Power and money is the nerve viewers comment on, and "back one superstar or ten thousand small workshops" is concrete.
- **"The many win when nobody has to do it alone."** This is the film's last word on the idea. Move it into the ending (fix 10), don't lose it.
- **The comment question** ("What small problem do you see every day that nobody solves?"). It harvests the forty-people plant, and there's no subscribe plea.
- **Honesty:** the film never claims the one-person billion-dollar company exists, never calls Base44 a one-person company at sale, and says the free programmes are untested. Keep all three.
