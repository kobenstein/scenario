## 0 · Hook (0:00–1:00)  ·  128 Wörter (~53 s)

Can superintelligence grow forever?

[Bild: Thumbnail exakt: leuchtende Kurve trifft gläserne Kuppel über der Erde, Risse aus Licht]

Not smarter. Bigger. More chips. More power plants. More robots. For everyone.

That's the promise. And it's being made out loud.

[Bild: Kurve schnellt zurück, startet neu und wird breiter / Chips / Kraftwerke / Roboter-Silhouetten, abstrakt / Menschenmenge im Licht / Bühnenlicht geht an]

[CLIP: Musk, Tesla shareholder meeting Nov 2025, ~8 s, about Optimus ending poverty / sustainable abundance]

Tesla calls it sustainable abundance. No limit named.

[Bild: ORIGINAL FOOTAGE vor der Leinwand „Sustainable abundance“ / Schriftzug zerfällt zu Licht und steigt zur Kuppel / Kuppel ohne Beschriftung]

But there is a limit. Not money. Not politics. Physics.

So here's the real question. What can grow forever, and what can't? And if something can't, does abundance come with an expiry date?

[Bild: Kuppel kommt ins Bild / Geld-Symbol prallt ab / Politik-Symbol prallt ab / Gleichungen glimmen im Glas / Kurve teilt sich angedeutet in zwei Linien / Sanduhr auf dem Wort „abundance“]

In the next ten minutes: where the wall is, why the promise misses it, and what it would really take.

[Bild: drei Stationen leuchten auf der Kurve auf: Wand, Prüfstand, Weg]

First, the wall. Imagine our appetite for energy just keeps growing, a little every year. In about four hundred years, Earth's surface would be as hot as boiling water. Not from carbon. From waste heat alone. Even if every watt were clean.

[Bild: Kamera fährt an die Kuppel / Kurve wächst Jahr für Jahr / Thermometer läuft bis zum Siedepunkt / CO₂-Wolke verblasst / Abwärme flimmert / Solarfelder glühen mit]

## 1 · Wie schnell es wirklich wächst  ·  145 Wörter (~60 s)

Four hundred years sounds safe. But one part of that curve is growing much faster than the rest. The part that feeds the machines.

By twenty thirty, the International Energy Agency expects data centers to use about twice today's electricity.

[Bild: Zeitleiste vierhundert Jahre schrumpft auf die Gegenwart / ein Strang der Kurve schießt steil heraus / Strang endet in Rechenzentren / Strang verdoppelt seine Dicke (IEA-Einblendung, 1,5 % als Text)]

Now here's the strange part. Every question you ask is getting cheaper. Google says one of its text prompts became more than thirty times more efficient in a single year.

So the total appetite should shrink. Right?

[Bild: eine Frage als Funke / Funke wird kleiner (Firmenangabe Google eingeblendet) / Waage: soll sinken?]

It doesn't. Economists call this the Jevons paradox. When something gets more efficient, we often end up using more of it. A British economist first spotted it with coal and steam engines.

Microsoft's chief executive, Satya Nadella, put it in four words. Jevons paradox strikes again.

[Bild: Millionen Funken, die Waage kippt nach oben / Begriff „Jevons paradox“ / Kohle und Dampfmaschine als Gravur, kurz / Zitatkarte Nadella „Jevons paradox strikes again!“]

Now you might say: nobody is promising forever. But look at the plans. No ceiling. Just abundance.

Breathtaking. But does physics agree?

[Bild: Pläne als Blaupausen, oben kein Deckel / Kurve ohne Ende / Blick hinauf zur Kuppel]

## 2 · Die Wand  ·  108 Wörter (~45 s)

The physicist Tom Murphy ran a simple calculation. Let our appetite for energy grow by just over two percent a year. That sounds tiny. But it compounds, century after century.

Keep that up for about four hundred years, and we'd need all the sunlight that falls on Earth. That's the boiling point you heard at the start.

[Bild: Rechentafel aus Licht / kleine Stufe pro Jahr / Stufen türmen sich über Jahrhunderte / die ganze Sonnenstrahlung fließt in die Kurve / Kuppel glüht, Rückgriff auf das Hook-Bild]

On the Kardashev scale, a type one civilization uses all the energy of its planet. We're not even type one yet.

[Bild: Kardashev-Leiter aus Licht / Sprosse „Typ 1“, Planet ganz erleuchtet / unser Punkt hängt unter der ersten Sprosse]

To be clear, this is a thought experiment, not a forecast. But it shows something real. Our appetite has a ceiling. And the promise you just heard doesn't mention one.

[Bild: Schrift „thought experiment“ wie Kreide / Kurve stößt an die Kuppel / Kuppel als Decke / Leinwand „Sustainable abundance“ ohne Decke]

## 3 · Das Versprechen auf dem Prüfstand  ·  320 Wörter (~132 s)

Which brings us back to that stage. Tesla's plan makes a bold claim. Growth in one place doesn't have to mean loss in another. Scarcity, it says, is a problem technology can solve.

That's exactly what physics just put on trial. So let's test it. Fairly. Four questions.

[Bild: zurück zur Bühne / Zitatkarte Masterplan [Wortlaut prüfen] / Waage im Gleichgewicht / Problem-Symbol löst sich in Technik auf / Kuppel als Richterpult / vier Karten verdeckt]

Question one. Where does the energy come from?

At that same meeting, shareholders approved a pay package for Musk. One of its goals: a million Optimus robots sold within a decade. A million robots, plus the data centers behind them. That's a lot more electricity. And the plan doesn't say where it comes from.

One Bloomberg columnist called it the flimsiest master plan yet. A huge promise, with no power bill attached.

[Bild: Karte 1 dreht sich: Blitz / Aktionärssaal, Hände heben sich / Zahl „1.000.000“ als Silhouetten-Raster / Rechenzentren dahinter / Stromlinie steigt / leere Zeile im Plan / Zitatkarte Bloomberg-Kolumne / Rechnung ohne Betrag]

Question two. Where do the materials come from?

With today's planned mines, the world could be short almost a third of its copper by twenty thirty-five. For lithium, the gap is even bigger. And every robot, like every electric car, runs on them.

Software scales. Metal doesn't.

[Bild: Karte 2 dreht sich: Metall / Mine mit Lücke von einem Drittel / Lithium-Lücke größer / Akku in Roboter und Auto / Code fliegt davon, Metallblock bleibt liegen]

Question three. Is the planet only about carbon?

A planetary health check last year found seven of nine planetary boundaries crossed. Fresh water. Land. Wildlife. Nitrogen.

Clean power helps with some. Not all seven. Carbon is one dial. The planet has nine.

[Bild: Karte 3 dreht sich: Weltkugel / neun Segmente, sieben rot / Wasser, Land, Wildnis, Stickstoff je ein Segment / sauberer Strom heilt einige / ein Drehregler, daneben neun]

Question four. Who is it for?

In a good scenario, Musk says, everyone gets a universal high income. Good scenario for whom? Whoever owns the robots decides who shares in the abundance.

[Bild: Karte 4 dreht sich: Hände / Leinwand „universal high income“ / Fragezeichen / Schlüssel zu den Robotern in wenigen Händen]

Now, to be fair, part of this promise is real. Solar and batteries really are getting cheaper, fast. According to the energy think tank Ember, battery costs fell by around forty-five percent in a single year. And Tesla builds some of them.

So the goal is right. What's missing is the way.

[Bild: Licht wird wärmer: Solarfeld, Batterien / Preisbalken fällt fast um die Hälfte / Batteriefabrik / Ziel strahlt, der Weg dorthin ist leer]

Which leaves us with a puzzle. If our appetite has a ceiling, is lasting abundance just a dream? Or have we been measuring the wrong thing?

[Bild: Zoom auf die Kurve / Sanduhr / Lupe über der Achsenbeschriftung]

## 4 · Die Wendung: Wachstum ist nicht Appetit  ·  166 Wörter (~69 s)

Here's the twist. Growth is not the same as appetite.

[Bild: Achsenbeschriftung trennt sich: „growth“ und „appetite“]

Think about light. The economist William Nordhaus traced its price back to ancient Babylon. Back then, one hour of light as bright as a modern bulb cost about a week of work. By the nineteen-nineties, it cost less than a second. Light became hundreds of thousands of times cheaper.

[Bild: Öllampe in Babylon / Arbeitswoche als Sanduhr gegen eine Stunde Licht / Glühbirne der Neunziger, Sekundenzeiger / Lichtpreis-Kurve stürzt in die Tiefe]

And that didn't happen because we burned hundreds of thousands of times more fuel. It happened because we got smarter at turning fuel into light.

Jevons noticed his paradox with coal. Intelligence can behave more like light. Its value can grow far faster than the fuel behind it.

[Bild: Brennstoff-Säule wächst nur ein wenig / Licht-Wert schießt hoch / Kohle und Licht nebeneinander / Wert-Linie läuft dem Brennstoff davon]

In recent years, thirty-two countries grew their economies and cut their emissions at the same time. The researchers warn it can flip back. But it shows the two lines can separate.

Superintelligence can grow forever in intelligence. It cannot grow forever in appetite. And that difference decides whether abundance lasts.

One curve becomes two. Intelligence keeps climbing. Appetite bends.

[Bild: Weltkarte, zweiunddreißig Länder leuchten / kleine Warnmarke / zwei Linien laufen auseinander / Leitgedanke in Licht / die Kurve teilt sich sichtbar: Intelligenz steigt, Appetit biegt ab]

## 5 · Die Abzweigung  ·  103 Wörter (~43 s)

So picture the same world in twenty sixty. Two versions.

[Bild: zwei Erden nebeneinander, zwanzigsechzig]

In the first, superintelligence makes everything cheaper, so we make more of everything. More things. More mines. More land. One planetary limit after another gives way. The abundance burns bright, and then it fades. Call it the furnace.

[Bild: linke Erde: Appetit-Linie steigt mit / Fabriken, Minen, Rodungen / Grenzen kippen eine nach der anderen / Glut / Kuppel wird zum Ofen, verblasst]

In the second, abundance flows where it costs the planet almost nothing. Knowledge. Health. Power from the sun. And where the planet is finite, in land, materials and wild places, we choose enough. Our lives grow richer while our appetite shrinks. Call it the ring.

[Bild: rechte Erde: Wissen, Gesundheit, Sonnenstrom leuchten / Land und Wildnis bleiben grün / Menschen in hellen Städten / Appetit-Linie legt sich als Ring um die Erde]

Same technology. Same intelligence. So which one are we building?

[Bild: gleiche Kurve über beiden Erden / Zoom in den Spalt dazwischen]

## 6 · Was wirklich nötig wäre  ·  438 Wörter (~181 s)

Let's turn those four cards back over. This time, with answers. And one of them points to a number that changes the whole question.

[Bild: vier Karten drehen sich zurück / eine Karte trägt eine verdeckte Zahl]

First card. Can clean power outrun the machines?

It's already racing. In twenty twenty-five, renewables overtook coal in the world's electricity for the first time, according to Ember. Clean sources covered all of the growth.

And data centers can be good neighbors. In a Phoenix test, a cluster of artificial intelligence chips slowed itself by a quarter at the grid's busiest hour. And nobody using it noticed.

So here's the rule. Data centers pay for their own clean power, and make room when your city needs it most.

[Bild: Karte Energie: Wettlauf zweier Linien / Erneuerbare überholen Kohle (Ember) / Zuwachs ganz grün / Rechenzentrum neben einer Stadt am Abend / Lastkurve, Rechenzentrum dimmt sich um ein Viertel / Bildschirme laufen weiter / Regel als Karte, Wohnzimmerlicht bleibt an]

Second card. Can we stop digging?

Not completely. But we can dig far less. Recycle what's already above ground. Put less metal into every device.

And even today's models can help. Researchers at a US national laboratory, working with Microsoft, used artificial intelligence to search through millions of candidates. They found a battery material that may need far less lithium. It's still in the lab. But imagine that search running on every material you depend on.

Then one rule makes the savings stick. Remember Jevons. Efficiency alone gets eaten up. So you need a ceiling.

America once capped the sulfur from its power plants and let companies trade permits under the cap. The pollution fell sharply, at a fraction of the feared cost.

[Bild: Karte Stoffe: Bagger hält inne / Schrott wird zum Kreislauf-Ring / Gerät mit weniger Metall / Laborbank / Moleküle fliegen durch ein Sieb / eines bleibt hängen (Forschung markiert) / Suche läuft über deine Alltagsdinge / Jevons-Waage kehrt zurück / Deckel legt sich auf die Kurve / Kraftwerk mit Deckel, Handelsrechte darunter / Schwefel-Linie fällt, Kostenbalken klein]

Third card. Can something watch over a whole planet?

Nearly every country on Earth has agreed to protect thirty percent of the world's land and sea by twenty thirty. A promise that big needs eyes.

Imagine superintelligence as its watchman. Reading every satellite image, every day. Spotting a patch of forest cleared in a week. Or a fishing fleet where it shouldn't be.

[Bild: Karte Natur: Erde von außen / dreißig Prozent Land und Meer färben sich grün / Augen-Symbol / Satellitenbilder Tag für Tag / Waldstück verschwindet in einer Woche, wird markiert / Fischereiflotte als Punkt am falschen Ort]

And now, that number.

A decent life for around ten billion people could take less than forty percent of today's world energy.

Less than half. For everyone. But only with two things together. Modern technology everywhere. And enough, instead of ever more.

[Bild: die verdeckte Zahl dreht sich um / zehn Milliarden Lichtpunkte / Energiebalken schrumpft unter vierzig Prozent / Ring aus zwei Hälften: Technik und Genug]

And enough doesn't mean a cave. Picture it for yourself. A warm home. Food on the table. Clean water from the tap. Electricity that never cuts out. Now picture that for everyone.

Today, not a single country gets its people there within the planet's limits. Which means the best version of this hasn't been built. By anyone.

That's the ring. Abundance in knowledge, health and time. Enough in materials and land.

[Bild: Du-Bild: warmes Zuhause im Winter / gedeckter Tisch / Wasserhahn / Licht geht an / Kamera zieht auf: Milliarden Fenster leuchten / Weltkarte noch ohne Vorbild / leerer Bauplatz im Licht / Ring: oben Wissen, Gesundheit, Zeit, unten Stoffe, Land]

And the last card. Who is it for? Abundance that only a few own isn't abundance at all. It lasts when all of us hold a share.

[Bild: letzte Karte: Hände / Ring teilt sich in unzählige Anteile, jeder Lichtpunkt hält einen]

## 7 · Ende: die Antwort  ·  89 Wörter (~37 s)

So, can superintelligence grow forever?

In what it knows, maybe. In what it takes, no.

[Bild: die geteilte Kurve von oben / Intelligenz-Linie / Appetit-Linie]

And that might be the best news of all. The wall was never in front of intelligence. Only in front of appetite.

The furnace or the ring. The technology won't choose. We will.

[Bild: Intelligenz steigt durch die Kuppel ins Sternenlicht / Kuppel stand nur vor dem Appetit / Ofen und Ring nebeneinander / Hand greift zum Ring]

We don't need a bigger planet. We need a wiser way to live on this one. And for the first time, we might have the intelligence to find it.

So tell us in the comments. What should superintelligence make abundant first?

[Bild: Erde mit leuchtendem Ring / Lichter der Städte auf der Nachtseite / Frage als ruhige Schrift]
