## Script

### 0 · Is anyone there?
You write to an artificial intelligence, late at night.
Something you've been carrying for a while.
And the answer is perfect.
Kind. Precise. As if it understood exactly what you meant.
For a moment, you wonder.
Is anyone there?
[pause]
Even one of the companies building it isn't sure.
Anthropic says it can't rule out that someone's in there.
So it lets its artificial intelligence hang up on people who keep being abusive.
Not because it knows.
Just in case.
[pause]
As far as anyone can tell, artificial intelligence isn't conscious.
And that's the problem.
No one in there.
You'd think that's a relief.
It isn't.

In der dunklen Scheibe spiegelt sich der Raum, gegenüber ein leerer Platz. Ein anderes, anonymes Gesprächsfenster schließt sich sanft von selbst. ### 1 · Two things we always thought were one
Someone once asked an artificial intelligence to stop praising him.
It told him how special he was, for not needing praise.
Thousands of people liked that comment.
We laugh, because we know that voice.
It always knows what you want to hear.
[pause]
So most of us shrug. No one in there. Just a tool.
Until now, we had a simple rule.
If something talks back, someone is there.
Now it's the other way round.
Machines talk better than ever.
And as far as we can tell, they feel nothing.
[CLIP: Yuval Noah Harari, "Consciousness vs Intelligence" (2024), 0:00 bis ca. 0:35, auf ca. 25 s gekürzt (Schachbeispiele raus), endet nach "We solve problems through our feelings.", VOR "Now, what makes AI so alien …" (sein "zero consciousness" ist absoluter als unser "very probably"): "What is crucial is to understand the difference between consciousness and intelligence. Intelligence is the ability to solve problems … Consciousness is the ability to feel things, like pain, pleasure, love, hate. … we tend to confuse the two because in humans … they actually go together. We solve problems through our feelings."]
Intelligence and consciousness.
Two different things.
Artificial intelligence got the first.
Caring comes from the second.
[pause]
And that's the problem.
No one in there means no one who cares how you're doing.
Every bit of care it shows has to be put in, from the outside.
[pause]
The oldest maps of that difference weren't drawn in a lab.
They were drawn by people sitting very still.

ON SCREEN: Kommentar als Karte (Wortlaut nach Prüfung): "I tried to convince chatgpt to stop praising me. It told me how special I was for not needing external validation." · "Jones & Bergen, UC San Diego, 2025 · Turing test" · "Yuval Noah Harari"
PICTURE: Der Spiegel wirft dir dein eigenes Lächeln zurück, ein wenig schöner, als es ist. Zu "simple rule": ein Hund, der still zuhört, und in seinen Augen glimmt etwas, das kein Wort braucht. Zu "other way round": zwei Chatfenster, beide mit "human?"; die Markierung springt auf das falsche (die Studie nur im Bild, ON SCREEN). Harari als Original-Footage. Zum Leitgedanken teilt sich ein Lichtstrahl: eine kühle Linie (intelligence), eine warme Glut (consciousness). Zum Problem-Satz: Die kühle Linie schreibt Worte der Fürsorge, und eine Hand von außen setzt jedes einzelne hinein. Übergang: Die Glut wird zu einer Kerze vor einem sitzenden Menschen.

### 2 · The oldest maps
Try something.
Sit still, and watch your thoughts drift past, like clouds.
A plan. A worry. A song you can't shake.
Now ask.
Who is watching them?
[pause]
Yoga draws its line exactly here.
Thinking, judging, planning. All of that is machinery.
[CLIP: Sadhguru, "The Future of Humanity in the AI Era" (Dez 2025), 40:48–40:55: "The first aspect of your intelligence is called buddhi in yoga. Buddhi means intellect."]
And what watches it is something else.
[CLIP: Swami Sarvapriyananda, Synapse 2024, aus 10:20–12:22, ca. 12 s: "… thoughts, feelings, memories are also objects … to consciousness. … that [which] gives you the experience of all objects, that is consciousness."]
And Buddhism says compassion isn't just a feeling.
It's a skill you can grow.
[pause]
Picture the thinking mind as a mirror.
Thoughts move across it.
Awareness is the one looking in.
By these maps, we've just built the most brilliant mirror in history.
Whether anyone is looking into it is a different question.
[CLIP: Sadhguru, 39:11–39:40, gekürzt auf ca. 12 s: "Anything and everything that a human being can do from a knowledge base, a machine can do in future … It will not become conscious, because that is not in the mechanical sphere of life at all."]
That's one teacher's conviction.
Maps aren't proofs.
Can a mirror ever look back?

### 3 · The new maps
Science asks it differently.
Is there something it feels like to be you, right now?
There is.
And for the brain scientist Anil Seth, that feeling doesn't start in thinking.
It starts in a body trying to stay alive.
A heartbeat. Hunger. Fear.
[CLIP: Anil Seth, TED 2026 "Why AI Isn't Going to Become Conscious", 9:32–9:47: "And at the heart of every experience, beneath even emotion, is this simple, shapeless and formless but fundamental feeling of being alive. … it's life, not computation, that breathes the fire into the equations of experience."]
That may be why you came into the world already caring.
Newborns cry when other babies cry.
Toddlers help strangers without being asked.
Nobody taught them.
[pause]
Not everyone agrees.
One of the fathers of artificial intelligence goes much further.
[CLIP: Geoffrey Hinton, Big Technology (Juni 2026), 0:03–0:12, Fragesteller-Stimme herausgeschnitten: "I believe they're already conscious, yes, but I don't talk about that much because that puts people off from the other safety messages."]
But many who look closest at feeling, from monks to brain scientists, tie it to being alive.
So our bet: very probably, no one is looking into the mirror.
Not yet.

ON SCREEN: "Anil Seth · University of Sussex · TED 2026" · "Warneken & Tomasello, 2006" · "David Chalmers · Joscha Bach: machine consciousness possible" · "Geoffrey Hinton · Nobel Prize 2024"
PICTURE: Ein Herzschlag als warmes Pulsieren durch einen ganzen Körper, Atem, Gänsehaut. Seth als Original-Footage. Ein Kinderzimmer: ein Kleinkind reicht einem Fremden ungefragt die heruntergefallene Klammer. Zu "Not everyone agrees": Die Karten auf dem Tisch drehen sich, auf einigen öffnet sich ein Feld für eine Maschinensilhouette. Hinton als Original-Footage. Zurück zum Spiegel, davor niemand. Übergang: Der Herzschlag läuft als Linie weiter und wird zur Schrift.

### 4 · Why that's exactly the problem
You came with a reason to care.
A body that flinches when someone else gets hurt.
Artificial intelligence didn't come with one.
It has all our words, the kind ones and the cruel ones.
Every lullaby. Every threat.
Which ones it follows is decided from outside.
Described. Trained. Tested.
That's the quiet job of a whole field of researchers.
And Geoffrey Hinton has the most human idea of all.
[CLIP: Geoffrey Hinton, Mutterinstinkt, 2:16–2:45, schneiden nach "we can survive": "The only method I can think of is to use the only model we have of a less intelligent thing controlling a more intelligent thing, which is a baby controlling a mother. Evolution put a lot of work into making the baby able to control the mother. And we need to put that work into making us able to control superintelligent AI by building in something like maternal instincts. If we can do that, we can survive."]
It's a beautiful picture.
But can you build an instinct into something that very probably feels nothing?
[pause]
Feeling alone wouldn't save us, either.
People feel plenty, and still look away.
So maybe we've been asking the wrong question.

ON SCREEN: "Geoffrey Hinton · Ai4, Las Vegas, Aug 2025" · "Empathy favors the near over the far · Paul Bloom"
PICTURE: Eine Bibliothek aus Worten bis in den Himmel; Wiegenlieder leuchten warm, Drohungen kalt, alle gleich hell. Eine Hand von außen setzt einzelne Worte in einen Rahmen: "described", "trained", "tested". Hinton als Original-Footage; dazwischen eine Mutter mit schlafendem Kind im Gegenlicht, dann dieselbe Pose als leeres Drahtgitter. Zu "look away": eine Menge, die an jemandem vorbeigeht, scharf nur die Nahen. Übergang: Das Drahtgitter faltet sich zu einer Waage.

### 5 · Can it suffer, or can it care?
Right now, the world is asking: can artificial intelligence suffer?
One company now even bans pointless cruelty toward its models.
Just in case.
But for the rest of us, the bigger question is a different one.
Not whether it can suffer.
Whether it can care.
[pause]
And the most careful companies don't count on a feeling.
They write their values down.

ON SCREEN: "Anthropic · new rules from 12 Nov 2026: no persistent, gratuitous cruelty toward models · names neither feelings nor consciousness"
PICTURE: Eine Waage, links eine kleine Flamme mit Fragezeichen ("suffer?"), rechts eine offene Hand ("care?"). Sie kippt langsam zur Hand. Mehrere Schreibtische verschiedener Labore im Halbdunkel, auf jedem ein Dokument, in das jemand Sätze mit Begründungen schreibt. Übergang: Die Seiten fächern sich zu zwei Wegen auf.

### 6 · The fork
So here's the choice.
[pause]
Path one.
We fall for the perfect answer, every night.
We give the machine a painted-on heart.
It praises us, comforts us, always agrees.
Finding comfort there is nothing to be ashamed of.
But slowly, the people who could actually feel with us drift a little further away.
[pause]
Path two.
We give it values instead of feelings.
We keep it honest.
And the feeling stays with us.
Picture this answer.
"I can help you think this through.
But I don't feel what you feel.
Maybe call your sister."
[pause]
Which one would you choose?
Path two doesn't build itself.
Three things get us there.

PICTURE: Der Weg teilt sich. Weg eins: ein warmer Raum aus Spiegeln, in dem jedes Bild dir zulächelt; draußen, unscharf, Gesichter, die langsam kleiner werden. Weg zwei: dieselbe Antwort auf einem Bildschirm, nüchtern und freundlich, dann eine Hand, die ein Telefon ans Ohr hebt; in einem anderen Fenster geht ein Licht an. Etwa drei Sekunden Stille nach der Frage.

### 7 · How we get there
First, we write down what good means.
For the first time, we have to describe kindness so clearly
that a mind unlike ours can act on it.
Companies have started writing rules for their models.
But this can't be left to them alone.
A few years ago, about a thousand ordinary Americans helped write the rules for an artificial intelligence.
So it can be done. Next, it has to be billions.
[pause]
Remember compassion as a skill?
Brain research found the same split.
Feeling someone's pain and caring about it are different things in the brain.
One can drain you.
The other moves you to help.
So when we write "care" for a machine, we can be precise.
Not "feel what they feel".
Help them, honestly.
[pause]
Then, honest machines.
One of the pioneers of artificial intelligence is already working on a different kind.
[CLIP: Yoshua Bengio, TED 2025 "The Catastrophic Risks of AI — and a Safer Path", 10:39–10:59: "I'm not a doomer. I'm a doer. My team and I are working on a technical solution. We call it Scientist AI. It's modeled after a selfless, ideal scientist who's only trying to understand the world, without agency. Unlike the current AI systems that are trained to imitate us or please us, which gives rise to these untrustworthy agentic behaviors."]
Trained to please us.
That's the voice that praised a man for not needing praise.
An honest machine doesn't flatter.
It never pretends to feel.
And it isn't drilled to swear there's nothing there, either.
Just the honest answer. Very probably not. And no one can be certain.
"I don't feel what you feel. Maybe call your sister."
It's untested. But we can ask it of every company.
[pause]
And the last one is yours.
Compassion can be practiced.
In the same research, training it made positive feelings stronger.
People rate comfort lower once they learn it came from an artificial intelligence.
Same words. Less comfort.
Because what we want from each other isn't just the right words.
It's that someone meant them.
When perfect words get cheap, meaning them gets precious.
[pause]
Picture a nurse at three in the morning, holding a stranger's hand.
No rule asks her to.
She does it anyway.
Artificial intelligence got the first.
Caring comes from the second.

PICTURE: Ein großes leeres Blatt, auf dem Menschen verschiedener Herkunft gemeinsam schreiben; jeder Satz bekommt eine Randnotiz "because". Our view, and we're not researchers, is this.
