### 2 · Three ways to share it: take, print or agree
There are three ways.
The first is to take.

Critics expect years in court, and it stops at the US border.
[pause]

Elon Musk wants government checks for everyone.
He's sure AI will make so much that prices won't rise.
Emad Mostaque, founder of Stability AI, wants new digital money tied to every verified human.
The world has done this before, for countries.
A few years ago, the IMF created reserve money worth hundreds of billions of dollars, in one go.
It went out by size and wealth. The poorest countries got a sliver.
[pause]
If prices hold, printing takes nothing from anyone.
But nobody ends up owning anything.
And whoever controls the tap decides who gets what.
[pause]
The third is to agree.
Before the fortune exists, we settle how it will be shared.
Like neighbors agreeing how to split the harvest before the first seed goes in.
It's the quietest way, and the one I keep coming back to.
[pause]
But it has one obvious problem.

Not each of us. All of us, together.

Plant a share for everyone, now, while it's still small.
Let it grow with every success of the winners.

### 4 · Step one: plant, now, while it's still small
Step one: plant.
Right now, these companies are still small.
OpenAI and Anthropic together are worth about two hundred and twenty dollars for every person on Earth.
If the fortune comes, they'll be worth many times that.
So the time to set slices aside is now.
[pause]
But a share only counts if it leaves the company.
In our plan, it goes into an independent fund, under a fixed rule that no board can change.
That's the difference between a gift and a right.
[pause]
The first seeds are already in the ground.
The US government turned its subsidies for Intel into a stake in the company.
Before taking office, South Korea's president floated a national AI company, partly owned by all its citizens.
American babies born these years can get a thousand government dollars, invested in the stock market.
None of this reaches everyone yet.
It stops at borders, and parents have to apply.
But every piece of it can be copied.
Picture a girl born tonight, anywhere on Earth.
A seed with her name on it.
By the time she can walk, it's already growing.
[pause]

Now picture the next headline: "AI company doubles its value."
Today, most of us scroll past and feel left behind.
With a share, their win is your raise. Automatically.
Envy turns into something closer to cheering.
[pause]
Pieces of this already exist.
The OpenAI Foundation was given about a quarter of OpenAI, and gets more if the company's value soars tenfold.
Oxford researchers proposed a Windfall Clause: the bigger the profit, the bigger the share given away.
[pause]
But who gets to cheer? Only the people of one rich country?
[pause]

PICTURE: The sprout becomes a young tree inside the glass fund. A spout on the fund pours a fixed slice into a long row of cups, one per person. A payslip on the kitchen table gains a new line: "your share ↑". Explanatory staircases: "OpenAI Foundation ≈ 26 % (2025) · more if value rises > 10× over 15 years"; "Windfall Clause, Oxford, 2020: 1 % → 20 % → 50 % of profits above thresholds". Faces watching the same headline turn from tight to smiling. Carry-over: the tree's first fruit.

### 6 · Step three: harvest, the same share for every human being
Step three: harvest.
One rule matters most: the same share for everyone.
Switzerland already does a small version.
Every resident, from baby to pensioner, gets the same refund from an environmental tax, every year.
It arrives through their health insurance.
Anthropic's plan mentions
QUOTE CARD: Anthropic, "Investing in the public benefit", 18 October 2026: "direct distributions to specific populations"
But someone would have to pick them.
An equal share needs no one to pick.
No board, no government, no algorithm decides who deserves it.
[pause]
This share wouldn't stop at a border.
So far, most plans in the headlines do.
A first step is being negotiated at the United Nations.
Countries could tax digital services their people use, AI included, even when the company sits elsewhere.
The United States has left the talks.
[pause]
The accounts already exist. Four in five adults have one, often on their phone.
But nobody pays every human on Earth yet.
The pledges depend on boards and billionaires.
And there's no global fund to fill those accounts.
That's exactly what we still have to agree on.
[pause]
So picture the day we do.
A care worker in Manila, at dawn.
A truck driver in Ohio, at dusk.
Two phones buzz at the same moment.
The same share.
Not charity. Their inheritance.
[pause]
