A.I. will end capitalism. Not in a hundred years. In your lifetime. And not the way you think.

Capitalism, in one sentence: somebody pays you to make something, then sells it for more than they paid you. Your work has a price. What you make has a price. The gap between them is profit. That's the whole engine.

Now watch what A.I. is doing to one of those prices.

Think about what you actually get paid for. Writing the email. Reading the contract. Finding the mistake. Explaining it to the customer. Most of it is thinking. And thinking is the thing that's getting cheap. Fast.

At the end of twenty twenty-two, the A.I. behind the first ChatGPT cost about twenty dollars for ten novels' worth of text. Two years later, the same work cost seven cents. And it keeps falling: about thirteen times cheaper, every single year.

Most people hear that and think: so A.I. takes my job, nobody earns a paycheck, nobody buys anything, and the whole thing collapses. A former Google executive, Mo Gawdat, says exactly that: the base of capitalism, "to hire you for a dollar and then sell what you make for two," is going to disappear.

And you can already see the pressure. This summer, America's biggest companies kept more profit from every dollar than at any point since the count began in two thousand nine. In the same three months, people who work for a living got their smallest slice of the pie since nineteen forty-seven. Bigger profits. Smaller slice for you. Same three months.

But that's not the real ending. The real ending is the price.

Capitalism can only make money on things that are scarce. Nobody sells you air, because there's plenty of it. Now imagine thinking becomes as plentiful as air. Then everything made from thinking gets cheaper, and cheaper, until there's almost nothing left to charge for.

A system built on charging for things runs out of things to charge for.

So where does the money go? Who still gets paid? And where does that leave you?
