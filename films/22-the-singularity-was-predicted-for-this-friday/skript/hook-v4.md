## 1 · Hook (Sprechtext)

[0:00 · Versprechen · Thumbnail-Bild: rote Tuschekurve rast auf das Kalenderblatt FRI 13 NOV 2026 zu]
The singularity was predicted for this Friday. November thirteenth, twenty twenty-six.

[0:05 · Beweis · Karte SCIENCE · 4 NOV 1960 · „Doomsday: Friday, 13 November, A.D. 2026“ („Doomsday“ wird nie gesprochen); die Formel zeichnet sich als rote Linie bis zum Kalenderblatt]
Not by a prophet. By a formula, in the journal Science, in nineteen sixty. It said the number of people on Earth would shoot up to infinity. Right about now.

[0:16 · Szene mit dem Zuschauer · die reale Linie (Anthrazit) läuft knapp über der roten; auf ihr ein Punkt „YOU“]
Sounds crazy. But for decades, the world ran right on the formula's schedule. Even ahead of it. If you were born before the mid-nineties, you were born ahead of schedule.

[0:27 · Wendung · die Anthrazit-Linie knickt ab, die rote schießt allein senkrecht hoch]
Then the line bent. The formula never allowed that.

[0:31 · die neue Linie · daneben steigt eine zweite Kurve steil an, Beschriftung ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE]
Now a new line is shooting up, faster than we ever did. This time it isn't people. It's artificial intelligence.

[0:38 · Hauptfrage · Rätselkarte WHAT BENT THE FIRST LINE? heftet sich an die erste Linie und bleibt bis ~1:35]
So what bent the first line? Whatever it was, it could bend the second one, too.

Not war. Not famine. Something you can see in your own family tree.

[derselbe Kreis, die Figuren werden zu Maschinen, die Maschinen bauen]

## 2 · Roter Faden (6 Stationen)

Motor der Mitte, sichtbar: Ab 0:38 hängt die Rätselkarte WHAT BENT THE FIRST LINE? an der ersten Linie. Bei ~1:35 ist sie gelöst (MORE CHOICES) und wandert an die zweite Linie: COULD IT BEND THIS ONE? Jede Station heftet eine Teilantwort an, und die zweite Linie biegt sich sichtbar ein Stück weiter. Keine Inhaltsangabe, keine Kapitelschilder. Bevölkerung endet bei ~1:35; danach höchstens ein Satz Rückgriff auf die erste Linie, immer im Dienst der KI-Frage.

**1 · Hook · 0:00–0:50**
- Frage im Kopf: How could a formula from nineteen sixty predict the singularity for this Friday?
- Teilantwort: It said the number of people would hit infinity right about now, the world ran ahead of it for decades (you too, if you were born before the mid-nineties), then the line bent, and now a new line, artificial intelligence, climbs faster; the bend was not war or famine but something in your own family tree.
- Schärfere Frage: What in my family tree could bend a line like that?

**2 · The loop · 0:50–1:35 (Bevölkerung endet hier)**
- Frage: What in my family tree could bend a line like that?
- Teilantwort: The formula rode a loop that runs through every family tree (more people, more inventors, more progress, more people, every round faster; today's singularity is the same loop with machines that build better machines), and the human loop broke at one link: kids survived, girls stayed in school, parents could plan, families got more choices, kids per woman fell by more than half, and more progress stopped meaning more people.
- Schärfere Frage: Machines have no family tree, so is their line headed for a date of its own?

**3 · The new line · 1:35–3:20 (ab hier nur KI)**
- Frage: Is the machine line headed for a date of its own?
- Teilantwort: Independent researchers time it, and the jobs artificial intelligence can finish on its own double in size every few months; a steady doubling aims at no date, and only if the doublings keep shrinking does the line take the old shape that does, which some forecasts assume.
- Atempause ~3:00: Why the thirteenth? The math only said late twenty twenty-six; the thirteenth is the birthday of the formula's lead author, Heinz von Foerster, reportedly his joke, and on Friday he would be one hundred fifteen.
- Schärfere Frage: Even its authors called their curve a projection, where a line goes if nothing changes, so who gets to change the second one?

**4 · See it · 3:20–5:00 (Akt 2)**
- Frage: Who gets to change a line that nobody can switch off?
- Teilantwort: Whoever can see it, because you can only steer what you see (the core of von Foerster's own field, cybernetics): the first line was visible because people counted it, census after census, and the second stays visible only while independent researchers measure it in public, with a measuring stick that keeps up.
- Schärfere Frage: But seeing a line doesn't bend it, and the first one bent because the choices reached billions of families, so whose choices will the second one widen?

**5 · Many hands · 5:00–7:00 (Akt 2)**
- Frage: Whose choices will it widen, everyone's or a few people's?
- Teilantwort: It lands well the way the first line bent: when its expert-level help (the lawyer you couldn't afford, the idea you shelved, a tutor for any kid anywhere) reaches billions of people, not just the few who build it.
- Schärfere Frage: But more buttons on a screen aren't more choices, so how can you tell whether something new makes your life bigger or smaller?

**6 · The birthday rule · 7:00–~9:00 (Finale)**
- Frage: How can I tell whether something new makes my life bigger or smaller?
- Antwort: Von Foerster wrote the test himself in nineteen seventy-three, "act always so as to increase the number of choices": after it arrives, do people make more of their own decisions, or fewer, including the decision to say no?
- Schluss: This Friday the formula says infinity, and there will be about eight billion of us, still choosing; the first line bent because people got more choices, the second can land the same way, and Friday is a checkpoint for both. Kommentar-Frage und Abo-Satz vor dem Schlussbild; letzter gesprochener Satz: "Happy birthday, Heinz."
